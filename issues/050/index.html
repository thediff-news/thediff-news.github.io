01  The Lean community blog’s joint statement, Nov 21 · Andrew Sutherland’s MIT verification page, updated 04:35 UTC Nov 22 · 897 HN points overnight

✅ Certified: the Lean and Mathlib communities sign Alpöge’s Claude-assisted Riemann Hypothesis proof.

A joint statement, endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative: “We hereby certify that both the statement and the proof of the Riemann Hypothesis are correct.” Independent kernels written in Rust agree. Two checks are still running.
SCENE

Friday morning this was a two-page remark on a personal site plus fourteen tarballs. By Saturday afternoon the Lean and Mathlib communities posted a joint statement, endorsed by the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative: “We have full confidence that Alpöge’s proof of the Riemann Hypothesis is correct, and we congratulate him on this achievement.” Dozens of people had reproduced the compilation within a day.

THE THING

Certified means four specific things. The statement is Mathlib’s own vetted definition of the Riemann Hypothesis. All 175,187 modules compile with zero errors. Two independent comparison tools confirm the proved theorem is exactly Mathlib’s statement. And the 61.6-gigabyte proof export - byte-identical in an offline sandbox rebuild - was accepted by Lean’s official kernel and by independent checkers: nanoda, a Rust kernel, in 27 minutes on 80 threads; con-leche and con-ron, which carry their own formal proof that they cannot accept a false proof. That answers yesterday’s caveat, Scott Aaronson’s worry that Lean’s logic itself might be the hole. MIT’s Andrew Sutherland ran the checks - with Claude Code, so Claude checked Claude’s homework - and his page still lists Lean’s default and paranoid kernels as running, no verdict yet. The axioms audit found only the three standard ones. Scope, from the page: five theorems covering RH and GRH for Dirichlet L-functions; Dedekind, Hecke and automorphic L-functions are not among them.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the verification ladder, not the theorem. One person with a good model produced more machine-checked mathematics in weeks than a field could review in years - and the field responded by building the review machine: a public challenge file, independent kernels, byte-identical rebuilds. That ladder now exists for any claim you ship: a checkable artifact, an isolated statement, a second implementation. What still has no shortcut: understanding. Aaronson’s point stands - mathematicians will spend years distilling why the proof works. Certified is not yet understood.

Watch  VERDICT

Update your language, not your roadmap: “claimed” is now stale - the correct phrase is “certified by the Lean and Mathlib communities, at the machine level, for RH and Dirichlet GRH.” The proof export, the challenge file and the logs are all public if your work touches formal methods. This fails you if you read certification as comprehension - the community certified the proof, not a human explanation of it - or if you extend the claim past its stated scope.

02  The quant’s Hugging Face repository, posted overnight · 385 HN points this morning · weights, runtime and drafters all public

🗜️ Overnight, one person squeezed Inkling 2 Mini into 5.4GB - and it still beats quants twice its size.

The LookAtMyQuant build compresses the 18.89GB text weights to 5.38GB - 1.65 bits per parameter - fine-tuned across all 24 MoE layers for Apple Silicon. The author’s harness: GSM8K 88.0% versus the teacher’s 96.5%, HumanEval 70.7% versus 92.7%.
SCENE

Yesterday’s issue shipped a 20GB laptop model. Overnight, a quantizer going by shinmegamiboson cut it to 5.38GB - small enough to run in about 6GB of active memory with a pair of 179MB drafting sidekicks - and posted the weights, the runtime and the eval tables before breakfast in California.

THE THING

The numbers, all from the author’s own harness: 1.65 bits per parameter across the text weights, packed for the MLX runtime on Apple Silicon. On an M5 Max he reports 1,807 tokens per second of prefill and 75 per second serial, rising to 124-143 with lossless drafted decoding. Quality, measured against the original BF16 teacher on identical harnesses: GSM8K 176 of 200 versus 193, executable HumanEval 116 of 164 versus 152, vision untouched at 4 of 4 - an 89.2% three-task mean at 3.5x compression. The comparison that matters: it outscores Unsloth’s 7.76GB and 9.14GB GGUF quants on both math and code while being 41% smaller. The honest limitation, his wording: calibration weighted English, chat, code and math heavily - for multilingual fidelity he points you to his 8.9GB to 12.8GB builds.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the size class. “Local fallback” stopped meaning “the small dumb model” this weekend: a 5.4GB file that keeps 89% of a coding model’s bench scores fits on the free space of a phone backup. If yesterday’s 20GB was a download you had to plan, today’s is an impulse. The harness is public and the eval tables name their splits - GSM8K test rows, all 164 HumanEval problems - so disbelief is runnable.

Try today  VERDICT

On Apple Silicon this is two commands and a 5.38GB download: install the LookAtMyQuant runtime, generate against one real ticket, compare with your cloud default. Treat every number here as the author’s until you rerun them - the harness is published for exactly that. This fails you if you need strong non-English fidelity - use the larger builds - or if you expect teacher-level scores at a third of the size.

ALSO ON THE RADAR

The rival human GRH proof now has a titled rebuttal - treat it as unverified too. A manuscript called “Invalidity of Masullo’s Proof of the Riemann Hypothesis” appeared on PhilArchive late Friday, and physicist Manlio De Domenico flagged it as “a counterexample to a key step.” Caution cuts both ways: the same author has a separate PhilArchive paper claiming RH is independent of ZFC, which the mainstream does not accept. Ravi Vakil’s update stands as the reliable status: problems reported, all of them must be resolved before the proof is accepted. PhilPapers: the rebuttal manuscript record →

OpenRouter off-boarded three inference providers and pulled 125 endpoints from zero-data-retention. After security researchers reported evidence that some providers were not upholding their data policies, OpenRouter removed the endpoints from ZDR eligibility as a precaution, notified affected customers, and says OpenRouter itself was not breached. Provider write-ups so far: Together disclosed prompt leakage affecting requests from June and July 2026; Novita posted a privacy notice about MiniMax requests; Phala posted a preliminary report. OpenRouter credits a Dragos report and is making the testing ongoing. OpenRouter: the announcement →

OpenAI shipped GPT-6.1 Terra: a mid-tier computer-use model at half of 6.1 Sol’s price. Their numbers: $1 per million input tokens, $5 output; Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 50.3% at maximum effort, more than double GPT-5.6 Terra; an internal error-inducing benchmark fell from 53.6% to 16.7% at low effort. OpenAI claims ~9x lower cost per task than Claude Opus 5.5 at max effort, and over three times faster task completion than GPT-6 Astra. Available now in ChatGPT Work, Codex and the API as gpt-6.1-terra. OpenAI: the announcement →
