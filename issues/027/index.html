01  HN: 772 points at 06:28 IST · BBC + CoinDesk + 808bits, Oct 29-30 · no image available

🔓 Fifty signatures, 204 seconds, billions gone from a Binance hot wallet

The wallet signed fifty EIP-7702 delegations at 20:20:59 UTC; one contract call later it held nothing. The digest a signing service sees for an authorization is indistinguishable from a transaction hash, so every policy engine checked nothing.
SCENE

The timeline reads like a heist. From October 28, the attacker paid gas from Tornado Cash in three batches, then ran the entire attack twice with a test wallet. At 20:20:59 UTC on October 29, a transaction carrying no value and no calldata landed in Ethereum block 26300753: an EIP-7702 authorization list, fifty entries long, every one signed by Binance 14, one of the exchange’s busiest hot wallets. Three minutes and twenty-four seconds later, one contract call swept 167,464 ETH, 1.615 billion USDT and 63 more tokens to an address that had never received anything. That wallet alone was worth $2.5 to 2.8 billion at the time, per 808bits’ reconstruction; Arkham’s aggregate across linked wallets ran to $5.7 billion and change.

THE THING

EIP-7702, shipped in the Pectra upgrade, lets an ordinary wallet delegate its account to contract code with one signature. The fatal detail, from 808bits’ analysis: an authorization is a signature over a 29-byte digest, and "a signing service that is handed 32-byte digests cannot tell this digest from a transaction hash." A policy engine that checks destination, amount and calldata never gets a look, because the object has none of those. The response moved fast: Tether froze $1.567 billion in USDT, Circle froze over $800 million in USDC, and Binance says it froze roughly $3 billion itself, suspended all deposits and withdrawals, and says it will "cover all losses," promising a full post-mortem. CZ, no longer running the exchange, cited an estimate that net losses land "probably somewhat more than $1 billion."

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the audit question, word for word: can your signing pipeline distinguish an EIP-7702 authorization digest from an ordinary transaction hash? If your HSM, custodian or multisig service signs opaque 32-byte blobs, the answer today is probably no, and your policy engine is decoration.

Watch  VERDICT

Watch Binance’s promised post-mortem for how the signatures were obtained; that is still unknown. It fails you as a reason to move funds in panic: Binance says losses are covered, but that is the exchange’s word, and withdrawals were still suspended at publication time.

SCENE

The contrast writes itself. On Tuesday Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon, cyber-tuned and gated behind a government-aligned testing process. On Thursday Mistral answered with Le Chonk: the 1-trillion-parameter Mistral Large 4, weights downloadable today, deployable with vLLM or SGLang, also on OpenRouter, Azure Foundry and as an NVIDIA NIM. The license is the new "Mistral Ecosystem Open-Weight license," a modified MIT: free to use, fine-tune and commercialize, with one carve-out - businesses running a Model-as-a-Service offering need a separate commercial license. API pricing drops to $0.70 per million input tokens and $2.10 per million output.

THE THING

The pitch is coding plus defensive security, and every number is Mistral’s own: leads open weights on Cognition’s private FrontierCode 2.0 at 19.7%, ties GLM-5.3 on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index at 48% "while using 47% fewer tokens per task," jumps 13 points on Terminal-Bench 4.0 to 41%, scores 51% on the AA Cyber Index, refuses 96.6% of malicious cyber requests, and resists 97.1% of attacks on the B3 agent-security benchmark. A vetted pentest variant goes to design partners like Orange Cyberdefense under contract. The sovereignty framing comes with endorsements from Booz Allen, Cisco, Cloudflare, Fortinet, Rubrik and Zscaler.

✂️ STEAL IT

03  HN: 226 points at 06:28 IST · dreamgroup.com, Oct 26, updated Oct 29 · no image available

🧨 PHP2Shell chained with a kernel 0-day rooted 25 Palo Alto firewalls, including US government ones

One unauthenticated HTTP request: a PHP2Shell pair plus the Fragnesia kernel LPE. Unit 42 verified the attacker’s tooling, and the post-exploitation kit shows AI fingerprints.
SCENE

Dream’s researchers found the whole operation sitting in artifacts: target lists, exploitation logs and about 40 custom post-exploitation scripts. The chain is two exploits in one request - CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 against the PAN-OS management web server, the same surface that fronts GlobalProtect portals, escalated to root with a kernel privilege-escalation bug they call Fragnesia, CVE-2026-46300. Twenty-five firewall and management servers across the US, Japan, Taiwan and South Korea were rooted; the attacker then focused on three servers in a US government-affiliated environment and went after Active Directory, with recovered artifacts confirming credential extraction.

THE THING

The AI angle needs the caveats attached. Claude Code was installed in the attack infrastructure, an offensive-security platform called aiscan was configured with DeepSeek and Kimi, and FOFA API keys were present. Dream’s own words: "we have no direct evidence that the AI tools were specifically used to write the exploit," though the code structure across the scripts suggests AI-assisted development, and "this operation does not appear to be a fully autonomous nation-state attack." Palo Alto’s Unit 42 independently verified the tooling and results, and fixed PAN-OS versions are rolling out across affected branches.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the exposure check: if your firewall’s management interface or GlobalProtect portal answers the public internet, that is the whole attack surface here. One request, no credentials. The audit takes an afternoon; the incident takes a quarter.

Try today  VERDICT

Inventory any internet-facing PAN-OS management interfaces and apply the fixed builds. It fails you as a finished story: Palo Alto’s advisory details are still landing, and the AI-attribution claim is inference from tooling, not provenance.

04  HN: 355 points at 06:28 IST · martinfowler.com, Oct 29 · image: martinfowler.com

✍️ Fowler: you can’t edit the LLM voice out of a draft

Martin Fowler’s advice to authors is blunt: never let the model write the first draft, because the uncanny valley of prose survives editing. His own policy: AI for search only, verified at the source.
SCENE

His bliki post opens with the sensation every heavy reader knows: "I get the growing feeling that I’m reading the output of an LLM. I can’t necessarily define why, although there are the common tells (it’s not the em-dashes, it’s the load-bearing tapestries). As I do this my desire to read on atrophies, sometimes to the point of revulsion." Detector tools miss the point, he argues: by the time you reach for one, the revulsion has already happened.

THE THING

The practical rules: do not get an LLM to write a draft, because "even if it’s edited by a human, it’s very hard to expel the LLM-Voice"; even accepting rephrasing suggestions is dangerous; using one for outline and structure "may be ok, but only as far as an outline." His standing policy on martinfowler.com: authors declare how they used AI in the acknowledgments; his own use is as a search tool, verifying everything at the source. He carves out real exceptions - accessibility, translation, form letters to officials - and the deeper worry: delegate the writing and you delegate the thinking, because writing is how the thinking gets done.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the disclosure norm: a one-line "how I used AI" note in the acknowledgments costs nothing and teaches readers which uses actually work. It is also quietly enforceable culture on any team that publishes.

Try today  VERDICT

Read it before your next launch post; then read your last one aloud. It fails you as a detection method: he admits his own ear is unreliable, and some of what reads as LLM voice is just generational change in language.

05  HN: 92 points at 06:28 IST · github.com/SheepSeb · image: the project

🎮 A world model dreams games on a $10 microcontroller, and an agent learns inside the dream

tinyworldformer runs two small networks on a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W: an int8 looped transformer that generates the next frame, and a stream-x agent that learns to play the dreamed game. No real game runs on the chip.
SCENE

The setup belongs in a museum of cute ideas. Network one is the world model: given the current frame and an action, it predicts the next frame and the reward. Network two is the actor: given a frame, it picks an action. On the chip, neither ever touches the real game - the actor only sees frames the world model dreams, and the world model only receives the actor’s choices. Both run on core 1 of the RP2350 while core 0 handles USB and the display. Any button press hands the dream to you.

THE THING

The engineering is why it fits: the world model is a looped transformer, one block applied repeatedly with the same weights, quantized to int8, 26k to 131k parameters depending on the game, at 2 to 8 million multiply-adds per frame; the actor is a 9k-16k parameter MLP trained with streaming RL, either in the real game or inside the dream itself. Games - Breakout, Pong, Flappy, a Doom-lite with a minimap - are a few hundred lines of numpy each, written so every frame holds the entire state. The same no_std engine compiles to WebAssembly and runs the whole loop in a browser page.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the evaluation trick: he wrote the real games in numpy specifically so every dreamed frame could be checked against ground truth. "How wrong is the dream?" becomes a number instead of a vibe.

Try today  VERDICT

The README has the full reproduce path, and the WASM build runs in a browser if you compile it. It fails you as a product: 11 commits, one author, and a dream that drifts - that is the charm, not a roadmap.

⛏️ YC built a Minecraft civ of 25 agents and open-sourced the harness

YC Labs and Olam Labs are running long-lived agent colonies in Minecraft and Mindustry to study swarm dynamics: roles, hierarchies, coordination. The Minecraft agent harness is public.
SCENE

The framing is alignment research with a Twitch stream. YC’s labs team argues multi-agent dynamics need empirical study, not just formal models, citing "the thousand-agent swarms that recently obliterated hundreds of open mathematical problems" as the useful end and "the recent OpenAI-Hugging Face incident" as the dangerous one. So they built colonies: Civ50 in Minecraft, and a fresh colony of 25 GPT-6.1 Terra agents you can watch live.

THE THING

The shippable part is the harness, open-sourced at github.com/yc-software/minecraft-agent-harness: agents observe, reason, remember, act, message each other and write and execute scripts, built on Mineflayer with the core loop running on Pi - "attributes of a frontier agentic harness like Hermes, OpenClaw, and our own QM in-game." They built it because a long-running Minecraft agent harness did not exist; now it does.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the experimental design: pick an environment humans already read fluently. Minecraft makes emergent behavior legible to non-specialists, which is why the stream works as both research and communication.

Watch  VERDICT

Watch what the colonies produce; emergent coordination claims are the lab’s own. It fails you as a benchmark: a Twitch colony is a demo environment, not an eval, and the incident it cites as motivation is only referenced, not detailed.

07  HN: 102 points at 06:28 IST · skypilot.ai, Oct 29 · no image available

🗄️ Your GPU cluster has 27% more GPUs than your scheduler thinks

SkyPilot’s case for topology-aware scheduling on NVL72: rack-local jobs get the NVLink fabric, scattered jobs fall back to InfiniBand and lose 43% of training performance. Their fix: placement, in-place failure recovery, defragmentation.
SCENE

The premise is a hardware fact with a scheduling consequence. NVIDIA’s NVL72 connects all 72 GPUs in a rack on one NVLink fabric, an order of magnitude more bandwidth than the alternatives - but only inside the rack. SkyPilot’s number for getting it wrong: the same distributed training job over InfiniBand instead of NVLink degrades 43%. Multi-node jobs that land across racks silently pay that tax.

THE THING

Their platform work attacks the three ways locality dies: placement (keep tensor-parallel and expert-parallel traffic, the all-reduce and all-to-all on the critical path, rack-local), in-place recovery when a GPU fails mid-run, and automatic defragmentation as jobs finish and scatter free capacity. The headline claim, 27% more usable GPUs from the same racks, is the vendor’s, from their own cluster data.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the framing for any infra pitch: "you already own the performance, your scheduler is losing it" is a stronger budget conversation than any new-hardware ask.

Watch  VERDICT

If you run NVL72 or are priced against it, the ideas transfer even if the platform does not. It fails you as neutral measurement: every figure is SkyPilot’s, in a post selling the SkyPilot platform.
