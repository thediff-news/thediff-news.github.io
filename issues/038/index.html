01  Anthropic + Artificial Analysis, Nov 9-10 · HN: 314 points at 06:27 IST

🚀 Anthropic says Fable 5.2 tops its coding benchmark - and tells most people to start with Opus 5.5 anyway

Anthropic, Nov 9: Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 are one model; Fable is open to all users at $8/$40 per million tokens, Mythos goes through application programs. Artificial Analysis ranks it first on intelligence and last on cost.
SCENE

Anthropic’s own account of its flagship demo: one engineer gave Fable 5.2 the task of designing a prototype operating system, starting from an empty folder. The model coordinated over 1,000 subagents, working around the clock for eight days, and produced a system Anthropic says boots on a laptop, with drivers, file storage, windows, a login screen and an installer. Their experiment, their description.

THE THING

What Anthropic published: Claude Fable and Mythos are "the same underlying model." Fable carries cybersecurity and biology safeguards and is available to all users; organizations in cyber or biomedical work can apply for Mythos through its verification programs. Both cost $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens (lower than 5.1, Anthropic says), with an optional fast mode at $16/$80 that it says is up to 2.5 times faster. In Anthropic’s own table, Fable 5.2 scores 49.1% on FrontierCode 2.0 at Xhigh effort against 45.5% for Opus 5.5, and at High effort roughly matches Opus 5.5 (45.3% vs 45.5%) for what Anthropic calls roughly half the cost per task. Anthropic’s own guidance: "For most coding and knowledge tasks, we recommend that users start with Claude Opus 5.5, our strongest daily driver," moving up to Fable or Mythos only for "the most ambitious, open-ended technical work." The outside check is Artificial Analysis: Intelligence Index 65, ranked #1 of 74 models, but also #73 of 73 on cost at $26.54 per Intelligence Index task, 46.4 minutes per task against a 12.6-minute median, and 275M output tokens against a 72.8M median - its words: "particularly expensive" and "very verbose." That is one run of one index; it is AA’s measurement, with Fable at Max effort.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the two-number habit: read the vendor’s best score and the independent cost-per-task side by side. Anthropic’s headline is the 49.1%; the number that decides your bill is AA’s $26.54 per task. If you run agents in loops, route the hard 10% of tasks to Fable as advisor or orchestrator - roles Anthropic itself describes - and leave the rest on a cheaper tier.

Watch  VERDICT

Skim Anthropic’s benchmark section and the AA page, and rerun one real task of yours on Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.2 before you switch anything. It fails you on latency: AA measured about 216 seconds to first answer token at Max effort, and the "ambitious work" claims (the hypervisor, the Fermat formalization) are Anthropic’s own, not independently verified.

02  Meta research blog, Nov 9 · HN: 266 points at 06:27 IST

🔥 Meta says Muse Ember matches Haiku 5.5’s best score at a third of the cost - its coding score is lower

Meta, Nov 9: Muse Ember is its smallest model, $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens on the Meta Model API. Meta’s table: same Artificial Analysis Index as Haiku 5.5 (36), lower on FrontierCode 2.0-AA (27.2 vs 34.9).
SCENE

Cline founder Saoud Rizwan, in a quote Meta published with the launch: Muse Ember is "purpose-built for, and excels at, subagent work like coding and document analysis," for teams cutting costs "by running it as the subagent in their harness." A partner’s statement, chosen and posted by Meta.

THE THING

Meta’s claim is that Ember matches the best score of Anthropic’s Haiku 5.5 at about a third of the cost; the numbers in Meta’s own comparison are Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index 36 for both, against 28 for Luna. On coding the same table is less flattering: FrontierCode 2.0-AA 27.2 for Ember against 34.9 for Haiku. Meta also published partner quotes: Box on contract and candidate screening, Cloudflare saying Ember ran "up to 30% faster at the tail" than the comparable model it tested, You.com saying it completed every coding quest with the lowest latency of any model it tested. All of that is Meta-posted partner testimony, not independent evaluation.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the subagent split: if your harness already has an orchestrator and workers, the worker is where a $0.10/$0.50 model earns its place. Run it on your cheapest, most repetitive subtask (grep-and-summarize, file triage) and compare to what you pay now.

Try today  VERDICT

Pick one repetitive subagent job and run it on Ember next to your current worker. It fails you if the task needs coding quality: Meta’s own table has Ember below Haiku 5.5 on FrontierCode, and an outside lab has not yet published its numbers.
