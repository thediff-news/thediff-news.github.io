How fast can you multiply two n-by-n matrices? Since Strassen’s 1969 surprise the answer kept shrinking, and plenty of experts guessed it bottoms out at 2. OpenAI’s internal model now claims the exact answer is 9/4 - a rational number - and backs the matching lower bound with public Lean files. 01  openai/math, Nov 23-24 · 1,030 HN points for the paper, 1,084 for Aaronson’s post

🧮 The answer is 9/4: OpenAI’s model claims the matrix multiplication exponent - with a matching lower bound, in public Lean files.

The same nights brought VP ≠ VNP from both OpenAI and Anthropic, a Fourier transform gate count below the FFT, and a thousand-point Aaronson post. Every claim is a lab claim; the certificates are on GitHub.
SCENE

Scott Aaronson had friends over for dinner Tuesday. They were still processing OpenAI’s and Anthropic’s proofs of VP ≠ VNP - hot on the heels of the Riemann proof that already felt like last week’s news - when, before dessert, OpenAI claimed ω, the matrix multiplication exponent, is exactly 9/4. A lower bound, matching the upper bound from the October 6 Mathocalypse drop. During dessert came another one: the true number of linear gates a Fourier transform needs is N (log N)^½, below the 1960s FFT. “It was as though the clouds had parted above Mount Sinai,” he wrote, “and we were waiting for the next sentence uttered by the Holy One Himself.”

THE THING

The claims, from the repos. Omega: the exponent of square matrix multiplication is 9/4 over every field; at every side length n≥2 at least ⌈n^{9/4}/2⌉ nonscalar multiplication or division gates are required, and the Lean development proves the square exponent for division-free circuits. VP ≠ VNP: OpenAI’s November 23 preprint, subsumed a day later by its family-379 papers over every field; Anthropic independently published its own Lean 4 formalization in anthropics/formal-math. FFT: upper and lower bounds, individual and batched, formalized. The openai/math repo now holds 730 manuscripts in 375 families, about 42% of top-line results formalized, produced by an unnamed internal model at roughly three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking compute per result across about 4,000 posed problems. Yupan Liu’s QIP(2) = PSPACE, a preprint that in 2025 would have been a result of the year, went almost unnoticed.

✂️ STEAL IT

Treat the lab GitHub repos as a new primary-source feed: openai/math, anthropics/formal-math and google-research are where results land first, papers and Lean files together, no press release. And read the scope documents before citing a result: the repo warns not all manuscripts are formalized and that “some of the unformalized results could have issues.” Aaronson, for his part, says he is “virtually certain, from multiple reliable sources” that the labs are sitting on crypto-relevant algorithmic bombshells - his sources, his words, unverified, and he declines to spread specifics.

Watch  VERDICT

If it holds, this is the decade’s algorithms story, and the Lean files make “if it holds” testable. Independent replay of the November 23-24 drops had not been published at press time - the Riemann proof took days of third-party compute. The failure mode is repeating a lab claim as settled math.

02  math.mit.edu, 22:05 UTC Nov 25 and 02:50 UTC Nov 26 · 336 HN points for the short proof

✅ The Riemann verification is finished - every check - and the 17-million-line proof now fits in 76,000 lines.

Andrew Sutherland’s MIT page flipped to “All checks listed below have finished,” closing the one check we had open since Monday. A day later, two shorter Lean editions of the same five theorems passed the same battery.
SCENE

Since Monday the last open item was the lake comparator (Lean v4.36.0) end-to-end run: paranoid kernel accepted Monday, nanoda Monday night, then a single-threaded default-kernel stage that took 48 hours last time. At 22:05 UTC on November 25 the page updated: “All checks listed below have finished” - the comparator on the canonical challenge reports “Your solution is okay!”, beside the byte-identical 61.5 GB sandboxed export and every kernel replay. The Riemann Hypothesis proof now sits behind the strongest public verification stack a mathematical claim has ever had.

THE THING

Then Anthropic made it small. lsernau/grh-lean, commit 72fead10, published November 25, holds two Lean 4 editions of the same five theorems: a “readable” edition of about 300,000 lines and a “compact” one of about 76,000 - 99.5% below the original 17 million. Sutherland’s second verification page, updated 02:50 UTC on November 26, shows both editions compiling clean, the theorems depending only on the three standard axioms, and lake comparator --paranoid reporting “Your solution is okay!” for both. The cost moved to the checkers: lean4lean needed 332 GB and two and a half hours on the readable edition - two hours of it on one definition - against 29 minutes and 7 GB on the compact one.

✂️ STEAL IT

The verification pipeline is now the product. lake comparator --paranoid - sandboxed build, statement comparison, axiom check, then every external checker in sequence - is a copyable pattern for gating any formal artifact in CI. Budget memory, not just time: the practical gate on the readable edition was a 332 GB lean4lean process, and the verifier ran on a 192-core AWS r8a.48xlarge with 1.5 TB of RAM.

Watch  VERDICT

The certification era’s first full lap: claim, audit, compression, re-audit, all in public, all in eleven days. The failure mode is treating “verified” as “understood” - Sutherland checks certificates, not exposition.

ALSO ON THE RADAR

1Password says an attacker reached its backend through an employee’s GitHub account - vaults safe, sign-in emails copied. Detected November 19, disclosed November 25 (1,255 HN points): the actor accessed source code and pushed malicious code to backend services, which downloaded names, sign-in email addresses, account identifiers, creation and last sign-in times, public keys, SRP parameters and tokens in emailed invite, provisioning and recovery links - for a subset of users. Source code and CI/CD were also reached; internal credentials are being rotated, and recovery codes were widened from six digits to ten alphanumeric characters. 1Password says vault contents and keys were never accessible, no customer action is needed, and to watch for phishing. How many users, and how the GitHub account was taken, remain undisclosed. 1Password: the incident post →

Nfs2root: a 12-year-old pre-auth remote root in the Linux kernel’s NFS server. CVE-2026-99362, severity 9.8 (106 HN points): anyone who can reach tcp/2049 can shrink the nfsd reply cache to zero and have the server copy a full reply into it - 61 controlled bytes past a kernel heap object, chained to a root shell, no account, no click, in every kernel since 3.16 (2014). Fixed in 6.12.117, 6.18.61, 7.3.2 and 7.4-rc1; patch and reboot, or firewall the port. OpenSec says its Antara agent found the bug and built the exploit on its own - the vendor’s wording, and a security product’s pitch. OpenSec: the writeup →

Google’s Cogentic posted six papers in a day - including integer factorization in L_N[1/3, O(1)]. The November 25 batch on the google-research repo (461 HN points): unconditional integer factorization in time L_N[1/3, O(1)], discrete logarithms in fixed-characteristic finite fields in sub-quasi-polynomial time, an exact polynomial-time algorithm for 1/5-approximate Nash equilibria, deterministic subexponential factoring, a square-root-barrier break for the log-rank conjecture, and unconditional edge reconstruction of linear-density graphs. The README says the papers were “produced by Cogentic” (arXiv 2609.40324), Google’s system; no Lean formalizations are listed, so every one is a claim until replayed. This is the cluster Aaronson’s bombshell remark points at. Google: the Cogentic papers →

🧪 ONE THING TO TRY TONIGHT
Ten minutes: benchmark your agent against Google’s 2015 code golfers.
Bin Jin revived 32 problems from go/codegolf, the internal contest he played at Google in 2015, with the archived scoreboards as the human records: each model gets 30 minutes per problem in each of eight languages and is scored on bytes, not passes (287 HN points). GPT-6.2 Astra is the first model to beat the Googlers’ records - his benchmark, his numbers. The problems, statements and test suites are public; the human solutions are never shown. Point your coding agent at one cell and watch it learn that passing is easy and short is hard. The failure mode is reading pass rates; the score is byte count.
