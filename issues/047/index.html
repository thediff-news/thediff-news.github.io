01  Anthropic’s official pricing announcement, Nov 18 · SEC registration effective Nov 18 · Nasdaq debut expected today under ANT

🔔 Anthropic priced at $83 - above its range, upsized, and the biggest IPO on record. Trading starts today.

980,000,000 shares at $83.00 - more shares than announced, above the $70-78 range. NPR puts the raise at a record $81.3 billion. ANT debuts on Nasdaq today; the first print is still ahead.
SCENE

Yesterday morning this was a clock story: pricing due during US hours, above-range chatter from unnamed sources (No. 046). Overnight it became a fact. Anthropic published the pricing notice itself - no anonymity required.

THE THING

The verified facts, from Anthropic’s own announcement: the IPO priced at $83.00 per share, above the marketed $70 to $78 range. The offering was upsized to 980,000,000 Class A shares - 956,351,354 sold by Anthropic and 23,648,646 by selling stockholders, whose proceeds do not go to the company - up from the 825,000,000 announced at roadshow launch. Underwriters hold a 30-day option on up to 147,000,000 more. The SEC declared the registration effective November 18; shares are expected to trade on the Nasdaq Global Select Market today, November 19, under the symbol ANT, with closing expected November 20. NPR reports the raise at a record $81.3 billion; AP describes it as the biggest Wall Street debut of all time at a roughly $1.5 trillion valuation. One thing that has not happened yet at our send time: the first trade. Every “debut” number you see before the Nasdaq opening cross is positioning, not a price.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the dilution math. Anthropic sold roughly 956 million new shares; at $83 that is about $79 billion of fresh primary capital - money the company itself says funds the compute build-out. Yesterday Nvidia’s release said Anthropic’s Grace Blackwell and Vera Rubin access through cloud providers was expanded; today’s price is what that access costs, financed. If Claude is load-bearing in your stack, watch two numbers over the next weeks: where ANT settles after the first-day pop, and whether the underwriters exercise the 147-million-share greenshoe - both tell you whether public money will keep underwriting the capacity your roadmap assumes.

Watch  VERDICT

Nothing to patch or migrate; this is a capital-structure event, not a product one. The useful habit: separate the pricing fact (official, $83, 980 million shares) from the valuation framing (AP’s $1.5 trillion is arithmetic on reported share counts, not an Anthropic statement) and from today’s open, which has not happened at send time. This fails you if you read a strong debut as a verdict on Claude’s quality - it prices the compute plan, not the model.

02  Google’s official announcement, Nov 18 · model card live · benchmark rankings are Google’s, from its post

⚡ Google shipped Gemini 4 Neon hours before the bell - frontier coding claims at $3 a million tokens.

One million-token context, #1 on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index per Google, rolling out in the API, AI Studio, Antigravity and the Gemini app for paid tiers. VentureBeat frames it as Opus 5.5-class coding at 25% lower prices.
SCENE

While Anthropic’s bankers closed the book, Google DeepMind posted Neon - the third Gemini 4 model in roughly a week, after Carbon and Hydrogen. Google’s own framing: Carbon for the deepest long-horizon work, Hydrogen for rapid everyday execution, Neon in the middle.

THE THING

The verified facts, from Google’s announcement and model card: pricing is $3 per million input tokens - $0.15 cached, a 95% discount - and $15 per million output, with a one million-token context window. Google says Neon ranks #3 overall with a score of 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at xhigh thinking, #1 overall on its Coding Agent Index at 58.6, and #2 on the Vals Index at 70.9%. Distribution is live now: the Gemini API in AI Studio, Gemini for business, Google Antigravity, and the Gemini app for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers - 9to5Google notes it replaces Argon in the picker, with 3.5 Flash-Lite renamed “Auto.” VentureBeat’s headline comparison - Opus 5.5-level coding at 25% lower API token prices - is VentureBeat’s framing, not Google’s claim, and “level” there is their read of the benchmark table, not an independent bake-off.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the eval protocol, not the leaderboard. Neon’s pitch is price per completed task, so run the comparison that actually matters: pick one real, annoying task from your own repo - a migration, a flaky test hunt - and run it in AI Studio on Neon against your current default. Time to a passing result times token price is the only leaderboard that bills you. Note what Google chose to headline: cached input at a 95% discount. That is a bet that your workload is re-read-heavy - long context, agentic loops - which is exactly where coding agents spend.

Try today  VERDICT

If you already pay for a coding model, an afternoon in AI Studio costs you nothing but the task you bring. Treat every ranking above as Google’s account of its own model until third-party evals land; the AA and Vals numbers are quoted from its post. This fails you if you switch production traffic on launch-day benchmarks - run your task first.
