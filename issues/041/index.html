01  Google blogs, Nov 12 · HN 489 + 347 points at 06:25 IST · AA page live overnight

🚀 Gemini 4 Hydrogen: Google’s speed tier beats Argon on the index at $1.50 in, $7.50 out - and Orchestrator swaps models mid-task

Hydrogen scores 51 on Artificial Analysis at its default thinking level, above Argon’s 50, reaching 54 at the new xhigh tier. Orchestrator in Antigravity hands one task between Carbon and Hydrogen as the work changes.
SCENE

Open Google Antigravity today and the model dropdown has a new entry: Orchestrator. Pick it and your task no longer belongs to one model. Carbon starts in a read-only phase - exploring dependencies, resolving ambiguities, writing a plan with explicit re-planning triggers - then hands execution to Hydrogen to write edits, bump packages and run tests. If Hydrogen hits friction, the task goes back to Carbon, the plan gets repaired, and execution resumes. Google’s pitch is that static routers guess a task’s difficulty from your first prompt and get it wrong as the task evolves; this is the bidirectional version.

THE THING

The numbers, separating Google’s from everyone else’s. Google’s: Hydrogen hits 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default high setting (Argon: 50), 54 at xhigh; flat pricing across the whole 1M-token context at $1.50 per million input and $7.50 per million output; against 3.8 Flash it is 2.5x faster with Terminal-Bench 4.1 at 57% versus 18.2% and fabricated claims cut more than 5x; Orchestrator reaches near-Carbon task success at 24% less quota and 19% faster - though Google’s own two posts disagree on whether that was measured on Terminal-Bench 4.0 or 4.1. Independent: Artificial Analysis put its page up overnight and confirms 54 at xhigh (#16 of 78), $1.50/$7.50, a 95% cache discount, and $3.16 per Index task - against $26.54 for Fable 5.2, the report-card story from two days ago. The counter-texture: AA clocked xhigh at 13.5 minutes per task (slower than the 12.8 median) and ranks it #76 of 77 for verbosity, burning 304M output tokens on the Index against a 72.8M median. "Speed and efficiency" is Google’s framing; the verbosity tax is AA’s measurement.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the architecture, whatever you build on: plan and verify on the expensive model, execute on the cheap one, and make the hand-off bidirectional - the executor needs a way to send the task back up when it breaks, not just receive it. If you are wiring this yourself, the Antigravity SDK example pairs Hydrogen triage-and-code agents with a Carbon agent reserved for refactors and security checks; the same pattern is one router function away in any harness.

Try today  VERDICT

Hydrogen is on every AI Studio tier, so a free key runs it: take one real bug, run it on Hydrogen at default, and price the tokens against your current model. It fails you if you read the launch claims without the setting labels - 51 is the default high tier, 54 is xhigh, and AA’s verbosity rank means long agentic loops can eat the price advantage you came for.

02  NVIDIA newsroom, Nov 12 · HN #1 at 3,145 points, 06:25 IST

🔩 Jensen Huang has resigned as Nvidia’s CEO, effective immediately - the board’s statement says everything except why

CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO and keeps the CFO job; Harvey C. Jones becomes the board’s first-ever chair. The statement never mentions the investigation the board announced two days earlier. Earnings are Tuesday.
SCENE

Inside Nvidia, a longtime engineer used the company’s internal suggestion box - before the resignation - to urge the board to weigh employee sentiment. Wired reviewed the correspondence: the post has drawn more than 1,600 comments, most of them arguing for Huang’s return. "If you believe in the mission," one anonymous commenter wrote, "bring back THE boss."

THE THING

What is board-confirmed, verbatim from the Nov 12 statement: "Jensen Huang has resigned as President, Chief Executive Officer, and a member of the Board, effective immediately." Kress - CFO since 2013, nearly 25 years in finance roles at Cisco and Microsoft before that - serves as interim CEO while the board runs a search open to internal and external candidates. Jones, the new chair, says the board expects "operations, customer commitments and product roadmap to continue without interruption"; Kress’s own sentence ends with "customers, partners, developers and shareholders." What is not in the statement: any reason, any mention of the independent directors’ investigation announced Nov 10 (Wachtell, Lipton and former acting SDNY U.S. Attorney Joon H. Kim retained), and any comment on the allegations outlets have reported this week. The split to keep: resignation, interim CEO and chair are board facts; the why remains reporting. The first public checkpoint is Tuesday Nov 17, when Nvidia reports Q3 FY27 results at 1:20pm PT with Kress on the call.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the reading habit: treat a transition statement like a changelog. What shipped - roles, dates, a named interim, an earnings date. What is absent - a cause, a timeline, a mention of the probe. Absence in an official document is a fact you can use: it tells you the board has chosen not to connect the two stories, which itself is the story to watch.

Watch  VERDICT

Nothing to do today unless you hold the stock. For builders, the load-bearing claim is Jones’s "roadmap continues without interruption" - CUDA, supply and the GTC cadence now sit with a caretaker and a search committee. The Nov 17 earnings call is the first test; listen for whether any analyst asks about the investigation on the record.

🧪 ONE THING TO TRY TONIGHT
Ten minutes: one bug, two models, one hand-off.
Open Antigravity (or AI Studio, where Hydrogen is on every tier) and give Orchestrator one real bug from your tracker - not a toy prompt. Watch for the Carbon-to-Hydrogen hand-off and, more importantly, the hand-back: does the fast model know when it is stuck? Then check the token math against what that bug cost you last time. If the answer is "it never handed back and shipped something broken," you have learned the same thing a static router would have taught you, cheaper.
