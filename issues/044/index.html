Muhammad Zulali needed his 32-year-old Jeep to pass California’s smog test. A $3 Arduino, two coding agents and some protocol archaeology later, his laptop reads - and writes to - the engine computer, and the repo is public.

And four days after Jensen Huang’s resignation, Nvidia’s board is caught between a Treasury Secretary demanding a “dramatic shakeup” on live TV and a “Bring Jensen Back” campaign by the ousted CEO’s allies - with Tuesday’s earnings call as the first on-record checkpoint.

Both stories are about who really holds the controls: the owner of the machine, or the institutions around it. This week, at least the Jeep owner won.

🖙 He pointed two coding agents at his Jeep’s 1994 engine computer. Now his laptop drives it.

Muhammad Zulali’s xj-scanner reads a 1991-95 Jeep ECU through a $3 Arduino Nano - protocol reverse-engineered by Claude Code and Codex, video published, repo public and climbing.
SCENE

The Jeep needed to pass California’s smog test, and its engine computer is from 1994: no documentation, no SDK, a proprietary diagnostic protocol older than many of the people reading this. So Zulali wired a $3 Arduino Nano into the diagnostic port and let Claude Code and Codex do the archaeology - sniff the bus, hypothesize the message format, test against the hardware, repeat. The published build video ends with the laptop not just reading the ECU but controlling the engine.

THE THING

The verified facts. The repo - “Read a 1991-95 Jeep’s engine computer from a laptop with a $3 Arduino Nano, and let an AI agent do the reading” - was created on November 10, is GPL-3.0, and passed 260 stars this morning. The video, “Claude reverse engineered my 32-year-old Jeep. Then it started controlling it,” is verified via YouTube metadata (title and channel; we read metadata, we did not watch). Tom’s Hardware (Nov 13) and TechRadar (Nov 14) covered the build; Tom’s puts the total hardware spend at $15, the repo says $3 for the Nano - both numbers are theirs. The Hacker News thread sat at 375 points and 191 comments at 06:27 IST.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the method, not the Jeep. Agents turn out to be excellent protocol archaeologists: any legacy box with a serial or diagnostic port and an undocumented protocol - old HVAC controllers, lab instruments, ebike batteries, a CNC pendant - is the same shape of problem. Capture traffic, let the agent hypothesize framing and write the decoder, test against the hardware, iterate. The human’s job is the wiring, and the judgment about what the agent may write to: reading a controller is safe, writing to one that moves metal or reports to a regulator is a decision, not a default.

Try today  VERDICT

If you own legacy hardware with a diagnostic port, the repo is a working template - hardware list, wiring and the agent prompts included. This fails you if you let an agent write to a controller you do not understand: an ECU that can be read can also be bricked, and emissions-related writes carry legal rules of their own.

02  FT, CNBC, BBC and Reuters reporting, Nov 13-15 · Nvidia IR: earnings call Tuesday, Nov 17, 2pm PT

⚔️ A “war room” wants Jensen Huang back. Washington wants an outsider. Nvidia’s board is in the middle.

FT reports the ousted CEO has discussed a comeback as Musk, Sweeney and two major shareholders lobby for his return; Treasury Secretary Bessent demands a “dramatic shakeup”; Megaspeed issues its first denial. Earnings Tuesday is the first on-record checkpoint.
SCENE

Sunday morning, four days after Huang resigned, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent went on Fox News and called for a “dramatic shakeup” at Nvidia: hire an outside CEO, and answer a question he said he keeps coming back to - “were they pressing these seemingly illicit sales to goose their revenues and their earnings?” Hours later, the FT reported that Huang has been discussing exactly the opposite with confidants: coming back.

THE THING

The split, carefully sourced. The comeback push is FT reporting attributed to people familiar: allies are assembling a “war room” of Silicon Valley executives to lobby the board and court the White House, and one ally says Huang backs it. Elon Musk and Tim Sweeney have backed a public “Bring Jensen Back” letter, and shareholders Brad Gerstner and Gavin Baker urged the board in a Friday open letter to keep “all options on the table, including the potential return of Jensen Huang.” Nvidia and Huang declined to comment; Huang holds no controlling stake. The CEO search is also FT reporting: the board approached Arm chief Rene Haas, one of several candidates. The board’s official statement says only that it is “in the process of selecting a search firm,” has not “formally engaged” any candidate, and that “speculation regarding individual candidates, whether internal or external, is premature.” In Washington, Bessent said several agencies “were in favour of putting Megaspeed on the entity list for months... But Commerce has the pen”; Commerce Secretary Lutnick has been called to testify before the Senate Banking Committee, and committees have asked his department to preserve Nvidia-related records. Megaspeed broke its silence to CNBC: Alice Huang “officially left Megaspeed effective August 30, 2025,” it has hired external counsel for a “thorough internal investigation,” and the media reports, it says, “are not supported by verifiable evidence.” Nvidia fell 13% last week, its worst week on record. The checkpoint: Q3 FY27 results and call on Tuesday, November 17 - written CFO commentary around 1:20pm PT, call at 2pm PT, per Nvidia’s investor relations - Colette Kress’s first on-record appearance as interim CEO, and the first since the WSJ financing account in No. 043.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the procurement read. The export-control question is now a cabinet-level fight with record-preservation letters attached: entity-list decisions, enforcement and Tuesday’s China and Singapore revenue commentary all feed the price and availability of the GPU capacity you rent. And an interim CEO, an activist shareholder push and a government demanding answers is roadmap uncertainty at the single vendor your stack compiles against. If you are signing year-long compute commitments this quarter, Tuesday’s guidance is data, not theater.

Watch  VERDICT

Nothing to patch or deploy. Watch Tuesday 2pm PT: whether Kress takes questions on the investigations and the financing book, and what guidance says about supply. This fails you if you plan around the comeback story - it is FT reporting the board has pointedly not endorsed, and the decision belongs to a board under public government pressure.
