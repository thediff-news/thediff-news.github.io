01  crissyfield.de research, with Handelsblatt · HN 703 points, 290 comments at 06:26 IST

🔓 Vibe-coded Lovable apps are exposing passports, payment data and 200,000 private chats

Security firm Crissy Field sampled 159,041 Lovable-built projects and pulled records containing email addresses from 30% of the sites it examined closely - using only the public Supabase key each site already hands to every visitor.
SCENE

A German lifestyle forum had more than 80,000 user profiles - full names, email addresses, dates of birth, gender - plus over 200,000 private chat messages readable by anyone, no login needed. Elsewhere the researchers found ticket and food orders with payment information, AI-generated summaries of a property manager’s internal emails, and, in their words, a particularly serious case: over 1,000 records with identity-document details including passport numbers, expiration dates and nationalities.

THE THING

The verified figures, all from Crissy Field’s own report. They identified 159,041 Lovable projects in Common Crawl data from December 2025 to September 2026; 34,545 used Supabase. Of those, 24,664 sent database records on first page load - not automatically a problem, since some data is meant to be public. But with further read-only requests using the same public API key, they found records containing email addresses in 10,560 tables across 7,403 of those 24,664 sites - 30%. The authors themselves warn this is not a confirmed leak rate: some records may be public contact data, and their Common Crawl sample is not representative. They downloaded only parts of datasets, publish no access details, and could not establish whether any data was misused. Lovable’s terms place responsibility for applications and data on customers; on November 10, after Handelsblatt’s first story, Lovable published its own security guide, “Find it, fix it, ship it.”

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the five-minute audit. Supabase’s anon key is public by design - the only thing protecting a table is its row-level security policy. For any app you have shipped with Lovable, Bolt or a Supabase backend: open an incognito window, watch the network tab on first load, and see what your own site hands a stranger. Then confirm every table has an RLS policy that assumes the key is public - because it is. If the app ever touches real customer data, get a human security review before launch, not after a researcher emails you.

Try today  VERDICT

The audit habit costs five minutes per app and the report’s methodology section tells you exactly what researchers - and attackers - look for. This fails you if you read “no technical knowledge needed” as “security included”: the platform builds the app, but the access rules are yours, and Lovable’s own terms say so.

02  WSJ (Gardizy & Whelan, Nov 12) · TechSpot follow-up at HN 317 points, 173 comments at 06:26 IST

🏔️ WSJ: Huang saw an “avalanche” of chips coming - and berated the CFO who now has his job

The Wall Street Journal reconstructs Nvidia’s customer-financing spree: credit guarantees for buyers who could not afford the chips, a $250 billion OpenAI backstop halved by Colette Kress, and a soup-vendor fable in Berlin. Reporting, not filings.
SCENE

This summer, Jensen Huang summoned his finance chiefs to diagnose a ballooning problem: Nvidia was in talks to guarantee billions for companies that could not afford its chips. Mid-presentation, he interrupted Colette Kress - named interim CEO on Thursday - calling her “a deer in the headlights,” according to a person familiar with the comments. The committed chip and memory supply coming at them, Huang said, was an “avalanche.”

THE THING

What the Journal reports, attributed to people familiar: the financing push was led by Huang’s son-in-law Nico Caprez, promoted to VP in January, with VP Raj Mirpuri, promising credit support within weeks in exchange for a revenue cut. For OpenAI’s Ohio campus - roughly $300 billion of Nvidia chips - Nvidia weighed a $250 billion backstop; the day after WSJ first reported that figure on July 26, Nvidia shares fell 5%, erasing $250 billion in market value. Kress reworked the deal and halved Nvidia’s commitment; the downsized deal signed August 17. Kress publicly defended the program in August: “We recognize the scale of this support, and we know some will call this circular financing. We see it differently. The equity returns on our invested capital will be excellent.” By October, Nvidia “started to turn off the tap,” and in Berlin Huang told chip-buyer CEOs a fable about a soup vendor with too many customers and not enough soup. Separately verified at the primary source: director Mark Stevens sold about $946 million in Nvidia stock in August and September; the SEC Form 4 for the largest batch (1,356,000 shares at $219.72 on Sept 18) is on EDGAR. There is no evidence connecting the sales to the resignation; TechSpot’s headline claim that Nvidia “deliberately kept GPU prices high” is that outlet’s inference, not the Journal’s wording.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the supply-chain read: the AI capacity you rent is partly financed by the company selling the chips, and the financier is now tapping the brakes. If your roadmap assumes falling GPU cloud prices, stress-test it - between wafer “avalanches,” consumer VRAM diverted to data centers, and paused credit deals, cheap abundant compute is a bet, not a baseline.

Watch  VERDICT

Nothing to deploy or patch. The first on-the-record checkpoint is Nvidia’s Q3 earnings call on Tuesday, November 17 - listen for whether Kress takes questions on the financing book and the investigations. Treat every vivid detail here as WSJ reporting attributed to unnamed sources until a filing or an official statement confirms it.

🧪 ONE THING TO TRY TONIGHT
Five minutes: check what your app hands a stranger.
Shipped anything with Lovable or Supabase? Open the live site in an incognito window, open the network inspector, and reload. Any Supabase request you see is one anyone on the internet can replay - the anon key is right there in the page. Copy one request, strip your login, and run it: if rows come back that should need an account, that table is missing a row-level security policy. Fix the policy before you fix lunch. While you are at it, Lovable’s own Nov 10 security guide walks the same audit from inside the platform.
