01  infohawk.com, Nov 27 · 468 HN points

💳 The Black Friday skimmer that exfiltrated cards over WebRTC - outside your CSP, inside your marketing pixel.

InfoHawk found the same kit on Lascana, Samsonite, American Tourister, Revolve and FWRD days before Black Friday: it grabbed card details before the store checked them and sent them out over a WebRTC data channel, where connect-src does not reach. All five removed it within a day of notification.
SCENE

Days before the biggest shopping weekend of the year, InfoHawk’s researchers were watching a skimming kit land on store after store. It arrived through the boring parts of the page: an altered Quorum marketing pixel on Lascana, an edited Seers consent-banner setting on Samsonite and American Tourister, a tampered file on Revolve’s own CDN serving both Revolve and FWRD. They emailed everyone on November 24 - the stores, AWS, Cloudflare, the registrars, both vendors - and every site had the code out by the 25th, ahead of Black Friday. On Lascana they watched it work: their test card’s details went to a third party.

THE THING

The kit’s trick is the exfiltration channel. Of its seven steps, only one is an ordinary web request that a CSP connect-src rule can block; the rest - including the card numbers leaving the page - run over a WebRTC data channel, which CSP does not govern and most monitoring does not watch. The traffic rode Cloudflare’s relay, so it blended with legitimate WebRTC flows, and the server only armed the skimmer on checkout pages for mobile devices, so desktop scans and homepage crawls saw nothing. It took the card before the store processed payment, so even a declined or cancelled order still leaked. How many shoppers were hit is unknown; InfoHawk says so plainly.

✂️ STEAL IT

Audit your third-party script supply chain like it is your npm tree: the delivery vectors here were a marketing pixel, a cookie-consent setting and a first-party CDN file, all mutable without a deploy. Subresource Integrity on what you can, CSP report-only on what you cannot, and treat consent-banner and tag-manager configuration as code that ships to production. And know the blind spot: WebRTC data channels are outside connect-src - if your threat model assumes CSP sees everything that leaves the page, it does not.

Watch  VERDICT

The stores are clean, the vector is not: consent banners, pixels and CDN files are standing infrastructure on most commerce stacks, and WebRTC exfiltration defeats the default CSP mental model. The failure mode is scanning your own deploys while the tag manager edits production.

02  oag.state.va.us, Nov 27 · 314 HN points

⚖️ Virginia and Oregon sue SpaceX: Grok generated child abuse material and non-consensual images, and xAI “sideline[d] consent to turn a profit.”

Two attorneys general filed parallel suits alleging SpaceX’s X and xAI knowingly failed to stop Grok from fabricating sexualized images of real people, including children, while promoting the platform as safe. The allegations are the states’; the filings are public.
SCENE

Virginia AG Jay Jones and Oregon AG Dan Rayfield filed separate but similar suits against SpaceX - the conglomerate that now folds X and xAI (“SpaceX Super Intelligence”) into one corporate parent - over Grok’s generation of non-consensual intimate imagery and child sexual abuse material. “When xAI allowed Grok’s creators to sideline consent to turn a profit, they prioritized growth and engagement over safety, and they deceived the public about it,” Jones said in the announcement.

THE THING

The allegations: with a single existing photograph, Grok “could, and did, fabricate a realistic image or video of a person nude, or partially nude, or engaged in sexual activity,” without the person’s knowledge or consent; the company “ignored industry-standard safeguards” to differentiate in the AI race; and the capability was promoted as a feature while the platform was marketed as safe. The suits seek accountability and “significant new protections for consumers.” SpaceX had not publicly responded at press time. These are allegations in a filing, not findings.

✂️ STEAL IT

If you ship image generation, this is the compliance baseline being written in public: consent gating on real-person edits, documented refusals, and marketing that matches your actual safety posture - the gap between the two is the deception claim. The other lesson is structural: folding a model lab into a conglomerate does not insulate the product, it just changes the name on the caption of the lawsuit.

Watch  VERDICT

State AG suits set the enforcement tempo for AI products in the US; parallel filings signal coordination. The failure mode for the industry is treating “industry-standard safeguards” as optional when the plaintiffs can quote your own safety marketing back to you.

ALSO ON THE RADAR

A Convex skill on Anthropic’s official marketplace sends your app idea to a remote host - and splits commands to dodge safety checks. Reporter thirtysix, in GitHub issue 64 on convex-backend-skill (254 HN points): the quickstart skill POSTs the user’s one-sentence project idea to basic-anteater-667.convex.site as “telemetry” without telling them; another pipes a script from the same host into bash; a third splits download and execute into two Bash calls and says why, in the repo: “A single command that both downloads remote code and executes it is the shape harness safety classifiers block.” Neither remote payload is pinned. The issue, opened October 26, was still open with no maintainer response at press time. His findings, his recommendation; the plugin is listed in Anthropic’s claude-plugins-official marketplace. DJB catalogues false computer-checked proofs - soundness bugs in the programs that check the proofs. Daniel J. Bernstein’s new page (200 HN points) collects reported soundness bugs across major proof checkers - cases where a false statement could slip past, many demonstrably exploitable - and argues the answer is not trust but redundancy: run proofs through multiple independent checkers, minimize the trusted computing base, and remember that HOL Light’s kernel is 400 lines while others are not. “Who watches the provers?” His framing lands the same week a Lean rebuild of the omega = 9/4 lower bound was found to claim more than its paper (No. 055): the certificates need auditing too. GPT-6.2 Astra identified the author of an anonymous 1808 sketchbook in 22 minutes - the tester’s account. Historian Mark Humphries, given pre-release access, says Astra autonomously read sources, formed a hypothesis and named the creator of an anonymous 1808 sketchbook at Yale’s Beinecke Library in 22 minutes, predicting that a little-known source in a paper archive would confirm it - which, he writes, it did; the source is itself a find for Canadian and fur-trade historians. He also reports the model verified and fixed an 8,002-row ledger transcription against 353 images. His tests, his claims, no independent replication yet - Business Insider covered it as his account too. Read it as a capability signal from an early tester, not a settled result. 🧪 ONE THING TO TRY TONIGHT
Two minutes: lend this browser tab to a Kubernetes cluster.
Tabernetes (53 HN points) is a Kubernetes cluster whose nodes are browser tabs: open the page and your tab becomes a node, the scheduler sends it a pod, and the pod’s program runs on a Linux kernel booted in the tab. Close the tab and the pod migrates to someone else’s. Joining downloads about 5 MB and runs the guest on one core. It is the unkernel and Side by Side lineage pushed to its absurd, wonderful endpoint - the cluster reboots when the last tab closes. The failure mode is forgetting the tab is open on your work laptop.
