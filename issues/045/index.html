01   Alibaba Cloud announcement, Nov 16 · HN: 855 points, 474 comments at 06:31 IST

🐉 Alibaba’s Qwen4-Max is live: 2.4 trillion parameters, a million-token context, and a busy HN discussion.

A 2.4T MoE that activates 71B per token, ranked sixth on Code Arena per Alibaba, about 60% cheaper per task than its predecessor on Alibaba’s own benchmark - with open-weight Qwen4 models promised “soon” but not shipped.
SCENE

In Alibaba’s own showcase, Qwen4-Max ran a 48-hour continuous agent loop - molecular design, synthesis and testing, active learning, four iterations - that ended in an experimentally validated coolant molecule for next-generation chip cooling. That is the vendor demo. The part developers could check within hours: the model went live on the QwenCloud API, according to Alibaba, and Hacker News voted the announcement to #1, where it had 855 points and 474 comments at 06:31 IST.

THE THING

The verified facts, with the vendor flags on. The announcement says: 2.4 trillion total parameters in a sparse Mixture-of-Experts design, 71 billion activated per token - 25% fewer active parameters than Qwen3.8-Max at the same total scale - plus a one-million-token context window and native vision. Alibaba says it ranks sixth on Code Arena, a third-party real-world coding leaderboard; we read the claim in the announcement and did not independently confirm the rank. On Work360 - Alibaba’s own benchmark across 721 industry task types - it says the model beats Qwen3.8-Max overall while using 35% fewer tokens at High effort and cutting average cost per task about 60%. Published pricing: $0.075 per million input tokens on an implicit cache hit. Available now on QwenCloud via the Token Plan and API, and in Alibaba’s QwenWork and QwenStudio apps. Open-weight, smaller-scale Qwen4 models are promised “soon” - not shipped.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the economics check. If your agent loops are cost-bound - long contexts, many tool calls, high effort - this is the a model worth testing against your current cost per completed task, and a million-token context changes what you can put in one call. The honest test is your own task suite: Alibaba’s 60%-cheaper figure is against its own predecessor on its own benchmark. And if self-hosting is the plan, the open weights are the thing to watch; “soon” is a promise, not a release.

Try today  VERDICT

Alibaba says the API is available and publishes a cached-input rate, so a cost-per-task comparison on your own evals takes an afternoon. This fails you if you need the open weights today - they are not out - or if your compliance posture rules out a Chinese-hosted API; in that case the watch item is the open-weight release, not the hosted model.

02   BIS interim final rule, Nov 16 · RIN 0694-AK50 · Lutnick testifies Dec 10 (CBS, citing a US official)

🌐 BIS targets remote access to restricted American chips through third-country data centers.

A Commerce Department interim final rule targets third-country data centers used to reach advanced US chips over the network - written, the release says, by and at the direction of Treasury and the NSC, mid-fight over who holds the “pen”.
SCENE

Picture a data center in a third country stacked with top-tier American AI chips. No crate crosses a border; the customer just logs in from somewhere the chips themselves could never be shipped. Yesterday the Commerce Department moved on the version where nothing physically moves at all.

THE THING

The verified facts. The Bureau of Industry and Security released an interim final rule - “Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors,” RIN 0694-AK50 - aimed at “remote access to advanced super intelligence (SI) chips and servers.” Per the release, it is “designed to ensure that existing restrictions on access to sensitive American technology cannot be circumvented through remote computing arrangements involving data centers in third countries,” and it aligns remote-access protections with the controls already applied to physical shipments. One line in the release carries the politics: the rule was “written by and at the direction of the Department of Treasury and the National Security Council” - striking, given Treasury Secretary Bessent publicly blaming Commerce for slow entity-list action (“Commerce has the pen,” he told CNBC). Separately, CBS reports Commerce Secretary Lutnick has agreed to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on December 10, citing a US official. What the rule requires of cloud providers and renters in practice sits in the docket, not the press release; we read the release.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the exposure check. The question export control aims to address is not only where the chip sits but who uses it from where. If your training or inference runs on rented capacity in third countries - especially through brokers or shell-tier resellers - check your provider’s compliance requirements rather than assuming that the server location alone settles them. Teams selling GPU-heavy services to overseas customers should read the IFR docket itself before their providers’ compliance teams do it for them.

Watch  VERDICT

Nothing to patch; a compliance surface to map. If export rules plausibly touch your compute supply chain, the docket (RIN 0694-AK50) is the read. This fails you if you treat it as distant geopolitics while your workloads run on restricted-tier silicon in a third country - that arrangement is exactly the target.
