🔧 DeepSeek open-sources its engine room

DeepInfer, the inference framework behind DeepSeek’s own production services, is public under Apache 2.0. 13,900 stars overnight.
SCENE

DeepSeek has never shipped the engine it runs its own models on. On October 16 the repo went up quietly; by Tuesday it was the top story on Hacker News. The README’s first line is the whole pitch: DeepInfer is the high-performance inference framework that powers DeepSeek’s large-scale production services. Not a research toy. The thing they actually run.

THE THING

Inside: the FlashMLA, DeepGEMM, DeepEP and TileLang kernels DeepSeek has been publishing piecemeal, now wired into one stack, plus DSpark speculative decoding with confidence-scheduled verification, sliding-window bounded replay, and a zero-overhead asynchronous batch scheduler. Two features stand out for anyone running evals or agents: batch-invariant inference, so results do not change with batch composition, and deterministic sampling, so identical input tokens plus a fixed seed give identical output. Supported models today: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash. Supported hardware: NVIDIA SM100 GPUs and Huawei Ascend 950 NPUs. The honest gap, printed in the README: a KV cache system is required for a complete service and is not included. This is offline inference with the core serving features, not the whole factory.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the determinism. Whatever stack you serve on, "same input, same output, whatever the batch" is the property that makes agent evals and regression tests mean anything. DeepInfer shows a production shop treats it as a first-class feature, not a nice-to-have.

Watch  VERDICT

Unless you own B200-class NVIDIA or Ascend 950 hardware, there is nothing to run at home today: those are the only two supported targets. Read the feature list as a spec for what serious serving now includes. It fails you if you expected a vLLM drop-in for your 4090.

✍️ The CA compromise proven with a signature

A researcher signs "We have a problem here" with the private keys of eight eMudhra intermediates. Browsers blocked them within days.
SCENE

How do you prove a certificate authority’s private keys are compromised, without a doubt, in public? A researcher identified only as Wayne chose the cleanest possible demonstration: he signed a message with the private key of each of the eight intermediate CAs. The message: "We have a problem here - Wayne." The proof bundle, with certificates and signatures anyone can verify in OpenSSL, is on the report site.

THE THING

The keys chain to roots trusted by the Mozilla, Microsoft, Apple and Chrome root programs, meaning an attacker holding them could mint valid-looking HTTPS certificates for any website. Wayne reported to the root programs on October 17. Mozilla blocked the certificates in Firefox through OneCRL in under three hours, no browser update needed, and shipped Firefox 158.0.1 plus ESR releases for stragglers the same week. Chrome blocked them from the 17th, Apple devices from the 19th, and eMudhra has revoked the intermediates. The open wound: clients that do not consume revocation data still trust them until updated. The report site ships test URLs: a certificate error from their two curl checks means your client is safe.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the disclosure format. A cryptographic proof of compromise, verifiable offline, beats a thousand-word advisory. Next time you report a key leak, sign something.

Try today  VERDICT

Two curl commands on the report page tell you whether your own tooling still trusts the compromised intermediates. Worth a minute on any machine that runs its own CA bundle: CI images and embedded clients are the usual stragglers. It fails you only if your curl links against a system store you then forget to update.

📞 Mistral answers the phone

Voxtral 3: transcription in 50+ languages, emotional TTS, and a voice-agent SDK, EU-hosted or self-deployed.
SCENE

Mistral’s launch page opens with a scene instead of a benchmark: Béatrice calls, explains in French that her doctor gave her a referral letter, and asks the agent to book it. Listen, reason, speak, act, in one streaming pipeline. That is the pitch for Voxtral 3, and the demo framing tells you exactly who Mistral is selling to: contact centers and assistants, not transcription hobbyists.

THE THING

Three pieces. Voxtral Small Transcribe 3 handles speech-to-text across 50+ languages with diarization, end-of-turn detection and context-biasing, where you feed it keyphrases, product names, jargon, to boost recall without fine-tuning. Mistral’s charts claim it beats Whisper, Scribe v2 and Gemini 3.5 Transcribe on FLEURS and diarization benchmarks; those are vendor numbers on vendor-picked cuts. A 4B Mini variant is priced at $0.0035 per minute. Voxtral Mini TTS 3 generates speech in 35+ languages and 50+ dialects, adjusting emotion and pace to context, and a Pro Voice tier adapts a speaker’s style from 30 minutes of audio. The voice agent SDK ties listening, reasoning, tool calling and speaking into one real-time stream. Deployment is the differentiator: Mistral’s EU-hosted API or your own infrastructure, with control over whether audio is retained.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal context-biasing. If you transcribe domain audio, a keyphrase list is the cheapest accuracy upgrade that exists, and it works without touching weights. Whatever vendor you use, ask for it by name.

Try today  VERDICT

If you build voice agents, the self-deploy option plus EU hosting answers the two questions procurement always asks. The failure boundary is the usual one: transcription claims are benchmark-table claims, so run your own hundred hardest clips before migrating anything.

🧅 Ten percent of Tor’s exits were stealing SSH passwords

One person’s ignored host-key warning unraveled a 333-node Ebury interception campaign.
SCENE

Friday morning, October 2, 09:35. A researcher connecting over Tor hits the warning every developer has clicked past a hundred times: REMOTE HOST IDENTIFICATION HAS CHANGED. IT IS POSSIBLE THAT SOMEONE IS DOING SOMETHING NASTY. This time someone was. His SSH session had been silently redirected to an interception server, and pulling that thread uncovered 333 infected Tor exit nodes, roughly a tenth of the entire network’s ~3,000 exits, running a coordinated campaign.

THE THING

The mechanics: attackers had planted Ebury 1.8.3, an OpenSSH backdoor and credential stealer, on the servers back in August. They turned compromised machines into exit nodes, port-filtered so their exits mostly carried unencrypted mail traffic, and redirected outbound SSH to 12 interception servers. Fall for it and the attacker takes your password, watches every command you type, then infects your server too. The researcher reported to the Tor Project’s network health team that Friday evening; their reply: "We’ve not seen an attack like that before." By Sunday the relays were flagged BadExit, and by Monday all infected servers were down. The dip is still visible on Tor’s public metrics.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the reflex: the host-key warning is not noise, it is the entire detection mechanism, and this whole takedown started because one person read it. Treat every unexpected fingerprint change as an incident until proven boring.

Watch  VERDICT

A field report to read, not a tool to install. If you SSH over Tor, verify host keys out of band, full stop. It fails you if you administer relays and have not checked whether yours were in the batch; the post and Tor’s lists have the details.

🚒 curl stops drowning by triaging in public

From October 26, low and medium severity reports become public GitHub issues. Only high and critical stay private.
SCENE

Daniel Stenberg does not do drama, which makes the language in his latest post land harder: curl’s maintainers feel "strained, overloaded and brought to the brink of what we can muster. We must act to survive." The flood is vulnerability reports, arriving faster than a small team can assess them, with no end in sight.

THE THING

The policy change is surgical. Starting October 26, reports deemed LOW or MEDIUM severity go to public GitHub issues instead of the private HackerOne tracker. Only HIGH and CRITICAL stay private. Reports sent to HackerOne that turn out to be below HIGH get closed as informative and publicly disclosed there. His number: over 90% of submissions over the last few years were quickly assessed as less than HIGH, meaning the private pipeline was carrying almost entirely public-grade work. Stenberg’s post does not say where the flood comes from; he calls it an avalanche and leaves it there.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the load-shed pattern. When a private queue becomes the bottleneck, redefine what deserves the queue. Severity-gating disclosure is unfashionable and honest: most "security reports" are bug reports wearing a suit.

Watch  VERDICT

Nothing to install; this is a governance change to borrow for your own project. It fails you if you genuinely need embargoed coordination for low-severity issues, but curl just priced that need at 10% of volume.

06  HN: 295 points · Peec.ai write-up, Oct 7, resurfaced Oct 20 · image: Peec.ai

📊 The 50,000 fake statistics ChatGPT cannot stop citing

A $50 experiment: one site of made-up numbers, deindexed by Google, still fetched by ChatGPT 72,000 times a month.
SCENE

Seven months ago Metehan Yesilyurt bought a fresh domain, stateglobe.com, and filled it with 50,000 statistics pages generated in a single day with GPT-4.1. Every number was invented. Total cost, including 15,000 listicles added later: about $50. Then he watched what the machines did with it.

THE THING

The split is the finding. Google buried the site almost immediately; impressions collapsed to nearly nothing. ChatGPT never got the memo. Checking his Cloudflare crawl data, Yesilyurt counted more than 72,000 requests in the last 30 days from ChatGPT-User alone, the agent ChatGPT uses to fetch pages live while answering questions. These are not training crawls; each one is a real answer being built on fabricated numbers. More than 330 domains now link to the site, including Shopify, Bluehost and DHL, and the made-up statistics have surfaced in an academic paper and a bank’s investment research. All of this is his own reporting on his own experiment, and he works at an AI-search analytics company, so apply the usual discount. The underlying mechanic, live-fetch grounding with no quality gate, is documented in his server logs.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the audit idea: query ChatGPT for your own domain’s topics and see what it fetches. If a $50 ghost site can feed answers for seven months, your sources deserve the same skepticism you would give a stranger’s spreadsheet.

Watch  VERDICT

Read it as one well-instrumented n=1, not a measurement of the whole web. It fails you if you walk away thinking only fake sites get cited; the point is nothing checks.
