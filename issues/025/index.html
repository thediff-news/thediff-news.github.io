01  Follow-up to No. 024 · anthropics/formal-math, Oct 27 · no image available

⚔️ Two labs now claim the Exponential Time Hypothesis is false

Anthropic’s formal-math repository holds a 2^o(n) algorithm for 3-SAT it says an internal Claude model discovered, plus a Lean 4 formalization. Hours earlier, an Anthropic mathematician had publicly questioned the originality of OpenAI’s claim.
SCENE

The README does not hedge either. "A deterministic algorithm solves 3-SAT on n variables in time 2^o(n), which refutes the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH)." The algorithm in fact solves the harder #3-SAT counting problem. The paper in the folder is bylined "by Claude (Anthropic)"; the repo says the result "was discovered by an internal research Claude model" and that the Lean 4 formalization was "written by AI models." And it prints its own caveat in the same breath: "The result is purely asymptotic and says nothing about solving formulas in practice."

THE THING

The sequence matters. TechCrunch reports that Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge first suggested OpenAI was basing its solutions on pre-existing work, and that Anthropic released its own refutation hours later. Scientific American reports that OpenAI’s release process violated a clarifying statement from AGMAI, the expert advisory panel OpenAI itself organized, and that OpenAI has tapped outside experts to vet its claim. The Anthropic formalization is real code you can read: MainResults.lean states the two claims as definitions on Mathlib’s Turing machines, and Solution.lean says it closes both from a counted-operations chain. What we could not do: compile it, audit the whole tree for placeholders, or find an independent qualified review confirming either lab’s claim. Both are lab-published results awaiting outside verdicts.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the receipt format. Anthropic’s repo separates the claims file from the proof tree, states the machine model, publishes the paper, and prints its own caveat in the README. If your system produces a claim you cannot evaluate yourself, that is the bar: statement, artifact, limits, in public.

Watch  VERDICT

Watch the vetting, not the victory laps: outside experts are reviewing OpenAI’s manuscript, and Anthropic’s formalization is public for anyone with a Lean toolchain to check. It fails you as settled mathematics: no independent review has confirmed either refutation, the priority dispute is unresolved, and on crypto implications all we have is an attributed comment from OpenAI science lead Dan Roberts that they checked and were "reasonably confident" nothing breaks.

02  HN: 848 points at 06:26 IST · ghostty.org, Oct 27 · image: ghostty.org

👻 Ghostty 1.4.0 shipped, and it is a monster

Seven months, 172 contributors, 2,889 commits, the first complete Kitty graphics implementation outside Kitty itself, and two security fixes worth the update on their own.
SCENE

The release notes tell you what kind of project this is before you read a single feature: they were hand-written by the maintainers, "painstakingly and lovingly," a combined 16-plus hours of work, with the full changelog and a security section up top. Ghostty 1.4.0 landed Tuesday with seven months of work from 172 contributors across 2,889 commits, large performance and memory improvements, and SSH integration improvements.

THE THING

The headline technical claim: a 100% complete Kitty graphics implementation including animation and relative placements, the first non-Kitty terminal to do it. The reason to stop reading and update: GHSA-4hwh-wx3w-3fqg, a paste-protection hole where carriage returns were not treated as line endings, so a paste could run a command without confirmation in programs that do not use bracketed paste, plus a malicious-graphics fix, GHSA-mhff-mv4q-fp63. HN kept it near the top of the front page all night.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the release-notes culture: security first, full changelog, written by the humans who shipped it, with the contributor count printed like a thank-you. It is why a terminal emulator can put up 800-plus points on a day dominated by AI news.

Try today  VERDICT

Update for the paste-protection fix alone, then read the notes for the fun of it. It fails you as a reason to switch terminals: the Kitty graphics completeness only matters if your tools draw in the terminal, and if yours do, you were already waiting for this.

03  HN: 792 points at 06:26 IST · deepmind.google, Oct 27 · no image available

🌍 DeepMind announced Genie 4, a world model that adds sound and dialogue

The new generation of DeepMind’s world simulator is natively multimodal: music, ambient noise and interactive dialogue generated alongside the world itself.
SCENE

The announcement post, from Raia Hadsell, Diego Rivas and the Genie team, makes the pitch in one sentence: "Genie 4 is natively multimodal, enabling audio-like music, ambient noises, and interactive dialogue to be simultaneously generated with the world." You set an event in motion, and Genie 4 simulates what happens next: how the world responds, how it sounds, and how the people in it move, react and speak.

THE THING

Under the hood, per the post, Genie 4 builds on the Gemini family: Gemini Omni anchors the simulations in real-world appearance and behavior, while other Gemini models supply the reasoning that keeps a simulation coherent. DeepMind frames the work as AGI infrastructure: safe, infinitely variable simulations for training and evaluating agents. What the post does not include, as far as we could verify: a public access program or any independent evaluation. The demo videos are DeepMind’s own.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the product framing: each generation of Genie adds one modality layer to the same world-simulation bet, and the blog post says plainly what the bet is for. Roadmaps read better when the thesis survives contact with the demo.

Watch  VERDICT

Watch for access and independent hands-on reports; until then every impressive clip is a lab’s own render. It fails you as something to build on today: no API, no weights and no evals accompanied the announcement that we could find.

04  HN: 463 points · Lobsters: 86 · discuss.python.org + LWN, Oct 26 · no image available

🐍 CPython is removing its experimental JIT

The Steering Council paused the project in June pending a PEP. The PEP never landed, and now the just-in-time compiler is on its way out.
SCENE

On June 5, the Python Steering Council posted "An announcement from the Steering Council regarding the JIT project": the experimental JIT merged for CPython 3.13 in 2024 would stay paused until its proponents produced a PEP describing how it could become a properly supported part of CPython. In that thread, Mark Shannon explained why the JIT was built inside CPython at all: the code-generator-heavy design is what makes it maintainable by volunteers, not just full-time professionals.

THE THING

This week LWN reported, and InfoWorld confirmed, the outcome: the JIT is being removed after its maintainers, Brandt Bucher and Savannah Ostrowski, missed the formalization deadlines. Python 3.15 final shipped on October 9 without the JIT ever leaving experimental status. The council’s June post is careful to credit the work: "the recent performance improvements are real and encouraging." The decision is about what a volunteer-run project can carry, not about whether the engineering was good.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the governance: a big experimental feature got a public deadline and a PEP requirement, and the project actually enforced both. Most projects let experiments linger until they become load-bearing folklore.

Skip  VERDICT

Nothing to do for almost everyone: the JIT was off by default and most deployments never enabled it. Watch what the freed-up core-team attention lands on instead.

05  HN: 435 points · IEEE Spectrum, Oct 21 · image: IEEE Spectrum

🧹 Wikipedia’s AI cleanup crew is still human

WikiProject AI Cleanup has hunted machine-generated text since 2023. IEEE Spectrum shadowed the volunteers as the tells disappear.
SCENE

Ellia Lebleu is a founding member of WikiProject AI Cleanup, a volunteer effort launched in 2023 to find and remove AI-generated material that breaks Wikipedia’s guidelines. When the cleanup began, Spectrum’s Eliza Strickland writes, AI writing was often betrayed by conspicuous clichés. That era is over: the text got better, and the volunteers’ job got harder.

THE THING

The story is the people: a standing crew of editors doing provenance work at encyclopedia scale, while the generation tools improve monthly. It is the human side of the same arms race the security teams in today’s fuzzing report are running, except the artifacts are sentences and the fix is a deletion log. HN sent it past 400 points, most of the thread arguing about where disclosure should live.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the operating stance: treat generated text as a supply-chain problem. Provenance first, removal when it breaks the rules, and no pretending the detector alone will save you.

Watch  VERDICT

Read the feature, then watch whether other user-generated platforms adopt the same crew model. It fails you as a doom story: the project has been at this for three years and is still shipping deletions, which is the more interesting headline.

06  HN: 187 points · cfsg.axiommath.ai · image: the project site

📚 A small team formalized the classification of finite simple groups in Lean, and found a hole in the literature

38.2 million lines of Lean, every logical step checked by the kernel, and counterexamples to a closure assertion used in a published route to a named theorem.
SCENE

The classification of finite simple groups is one of the great collective achievements of mathematics: a first-generation proof spanning thousands of journal pages, written by about a hundred mathematicians over several decades. The site that went up this week presents a complete formalization of it in Lean, following the first-generation proof, with every logical step checked by Lean’s kernel: 38.2 million lines, 2.05 million declarations, 102 milestones, 488 source papers and books. The core contributors listed are Dean Cureton, Vasily Ilin, Bhavik Mehta and Niels Voss.

THE THING

The finding inside the formalization is the story. While formalizing the first-generation proof, the team found counterexamples to a closure assertion used in a published route to the Unbalanced Group Theorem; an accompanying paper explains the issue and the alternative route their proof takes. That is the formal-methods pitch working exactly as advertised: the machine read the literature, and the literature had a hole. Keep the same discipline as today’s lead: this is the project’s own site and paper, and the proof is public for anyone with a Lean toolchain to audit.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the errata culture: the site puts an Errata page in the top navigation next to the proof map. If your project claims machine-checked correctness, the corrections ledger belongs on the homepage.

Watch  VERDICT

Dig in if formal mathematics is yours; for everyone else, the number to remember is 38.2 million lines, and the fact that the hole surfaced because someone finally made the proof machine-readable.

The claims are the maker’s and we did not install or test it, but the design alone is worth the points it collected: citation-first answers from named official manuals, offline by construction.

08  Lobsters discussion · projectzero.google, Oct 27 · image: Project Zero

🐛 Project Zero scored it: AI agent 45, fuzzer 37

Six months, same JavaScript engine, same reporting pipeline. BigSleep found more V8 vulnerabilities than Fuzzilli, and a substantial share of each side’s bugs were invisible to the other.
SCENE

Google’s Project Zero ran the comparison everyone argues about and almost nobody runs cleanly. Same target, Chrome’s V8 engine; same window, the past six months; same pipeline, bugs reported through Clusterfuzz into Chrome’s issue tracker. Fuzzilli, the special-purpose fuzzer, runs continuously. BigSleep, the LLM agent, runs once a day over new commits and writes proof-of-concept testcases for what it finds. Samuel Groß wrote it up.

THE THING

The scoreboard: BigSleep found more vulnerabilities than Fuzzilli, 45 to 37, with a 25% overlap in the specific bugs reported, and the authors caution the methodology tends to underestimate overlap. The sentence that matters: a substantial portion of vulnerabilities were discoverable by only LLMs or only fuzzing. Project Zero’s recommendation is not a coronation: fuzzing and LLMs are complementary, and vendors should run both.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the methodology: two systems in production on the same code with the same reporting path are fairly directly comparable, and the write-up says where the comparison is weak before you can ask.

Try today  VERDICT

If you own a fuzzing pipeline, add an agentic pass over new commits; if you own an agent pipeline, keep the fuzzer. It fails you as a ranking: 45-to-37 is Google’s own measurement on one engine, with the overlap caveats stated by the authors themselves.
