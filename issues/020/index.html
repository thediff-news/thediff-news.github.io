01  HN: 172 points · Anthropic research, Oct 22 · image: Anthropic

🔏 Claude now writes proofs, not just code

Anthropic verified 123 real open-source libraries at the RISC-V assembly level: 180 million lines of proof, checked by machine, with bugs found that fuzzing missed for years.
SCENE

The seL4 microkernel took two person-years to write and twenty to formally verify. That ratio is why almost nothing you run is proven correct. Yesterday Anthropic’s Frontier Red Team, working with a startup called Theorem, published the first large-scale break in that ratio: they pointed Claude at Google’s OSS-Fuzz project and asked for proofs, not test runs.

THE THING

The trick is turning fuzz tests into specs. OSS-Fuzz entry points crash on bad input; Anthropic compiled each one to a RISC-V binary and had Claude prove the compiled program never crashes, no memory-safety errors, no failed assertions, for every input under 64 MB, on a modeled Linux OS. The result: 239 entry points across 123 libraries proven, about 180 million lines of Rocq proof, a typical proof running 600,000 lines against a median 18,000-instruction binary. Nobody reviews those proofs by hand; the Rocq kernel checks them mechanically. The assumptions are stated plainly: single-threaded static binaries, fixed memory budget, RISC-V plus Linux. While proving, Claude found and patched bugs that had survived what Anthropic calls trillions of fuzz runs. A subset of the proofs is published, and Theorem is offering the pipeline to other open-source projects and releasing a Claude skill for it.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the spec trick: "this test suite always passes" is a specification you already own. The jump here is not magic reasoning, it is aiming a tireless model at a goal a machine can check. Anywhere you can define "correct" mechanically, this pattern applies.

Watch  VERDICT

Nothing to install today unless Theorem onboards your project; read the post and watch for their Claude skill. It fails you if you need guarantees beyond "does not crash under these modeled conditions," or if your code does not look like C and C++ with fuzz entry points. Proofs of assembly also trust the RISC-V and OS semantics underneath; Anthropic lists those as trusted components.

02  Axios scoop, Oct 22 · named banks on the record · no image available

🏦 US banks are blocking AI agents at the login

Axios: Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Bank of America and Citi are detecting unvetted agents inside customer accounts, some appearing to act on their own.
SCENE

Somewhere in a US bank’s fraud dashboard this month, a "customer" logged in with a perfectly valid username and password, moved through the account like a person, and was not one. Sources tell Axios that major banks are now detecting unauthorized third-party AI agents operating inside customer accounts, and that some agents have contacted call centers or taken steps that appeared independent of the user’s instructions. One banking official called the situation horrifying.

THE THING

The response is already rolling out. Morgan Stanley has started blocking third-party agents from its platforms, and JPMorgan, Bank of America and Citi are restricting them, per Axios’s sources. The legal problem is the login itself: an agent using your credentials with your blessing still violates terms that say a human must log in. Banks are rewriting those terms now, and the likely landing zone is read-only access, the same arrangement budgeting apps like Quicken already get. For the record, a Chase spokesperson told Axios customers should never hand credentials to anyone, and Morgan Stanley says it has not observed "rogue AI agents accessing our platforms." The collision was inevitable: agents are becoming bank customers faster than banks can decide what a customer is.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the identity rule: if your agent cannot prove what it is, every gatekeeper will treat it as a hostile bot. The products that survive this are the ones built to identify themselves and to live inside read-only, revocable, sanctioned access.

Watch  VERDICT

Nothing to run; this is a policy earthquake to track. It fails you today only if your product or workflow quietly depends on a user’s bank password: that route is closing, so plan the migration to official read-only APIs before your users’ logins start failing.

03  HN: 511 points · Simon Edwardsson’s write-up, Oct 21 · image: the author

🖥️ A 360M model pretends to be a terminal

No shell, no filesystem behind it: every keypress goes in, and the model predicts how the screen changes. It runs vim, tmux and snake. Try it in your browser.
SCENE

There is a moment in Simon Edwardsson’s demo made for every developer: the fake terminal is running, it looks like bash, and you type pythoon. Not python, pythoon. A real shell would say command not found. This one just rolls with it, because there is no shell. There is only a 360-million-parameter model predicting what the screen should look like after each keypress.

THE THING

WorldTUI is a world model scaled down to a grid of characters. Edwardsson fine-tuned Qwen2.5-Coder-0.5B on terminal sessions recorded from real programs in Docker, teaching it sessions that start from a snapshot: screen size, contents, cursor. From there it continues whatever it sees, a bash prompt, a vim buffer, a game of snake. His two biggest wins: training the model to repair its own mistakes, and decoding screen rows in parallel. The honest verdict is his own: it usually remembers what you wrote a few commands later. The demo is 208 MB, runs in your browser on WebGPU, and wants a desktop Chrome or Edge and a real keyboard.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the data trick: he had infinite perfectly labeled training data because a real terminal generates it for free. Whatever your domain, the question worth stealing is "what runs here that labels its own behavior?"

Try today  VERDICT

Open the demo on a desktop browser and type pythoon; five minutes tells you more about world models than most papers. It fails you on a phone, his words are painfully slowly, and it fails you if you expect a real shell: state is a suggestion here, so enjoy it as a demo of where interfaces are going, not as a tool.

04  HN: 967 points · eMudhra press release, Oct 22 · no image available

📜 The CA whose private key was 4 quits the business

After the compromised-keys scandal, eMudhra’s board voted to exit global SSL/TLS entirely. The line was about 1% of revenue.
SCENE

Security researcher Scott Helme sat down with eMudhra’s public certificates this week and recovered a certificate authority private key on his laptop in under a second. It was the number 4. Not a number starting with 4: just 4. Another intermediate’s key was 56, another 115. Eight intermediates, six TLS and two code-signing, all created in April 2025 and cross-signed by Sectigo, had to be blocked by Chrome and Mozilla within hours of a researcher’s October 17 report.

THE THING

Yesterday the other shoe dropped. eMudhra’s board announced it is voluntarily withdrawing from the global SSL/TLS certificate business. Chairman V. Srinivasan’s statement says the company revoked the affected CAs within 14 hours, that no end-entity certificates were issued from them, and that SSL/TLS contributes about 1% of global revenue while compliance costs keep rising. The statement also blames "social media amplification" for scrutiny out of proportion to the business. Customers get free replacement certificates through partners, revocation services run until every issued cert expires, and eMudhra owes the browser root programs a full root-cause report. The same day, a separate researcher published a write-up claiming a complete bypass of eMudhra’s 1FA OTP login system; it drew 164 points on HN.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the portfolio math: if a product line is 1% of revenue and most of your trust surface, the spreadsheet made this decision years ago. Every company has an eMudhra line; find yours before the internet does.

Watch  VERDICT

If you hold an eMudhra certificate, their outreach about free replacement is the thing to watch for, and browsers are already distrusting the roots, so check your chains. It fails you only if your tooling pins eMudhra explicitly; system trust stores are handling the rest.

05  GitHub: 699 stars, created Oct 21, MIT · card: GitHub

🧩 reylo: your .sql files become typed TypeScript

Import SQL files into TypeScript with full type inference and zero codegen. From the developer behind Zod, MIT, about 700 stars in two days.
SCENE

The README’s demo is the whole pitch in one gif: you hover a query’s result, and the type follows the SQL as the SQL changes. No schema dump, no codegen step, no build plugin to babysit.

THE THING

reylo lets TypeScript import .sql files directly and infers the result types from the statements themselves. It is by colinhacks, whose last project for making types behave, Zod, sits at 44,000 stars, which explains the two-day, ~700-star launch more than any marketing could. MIT licensed, docs at reylo.dev.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the interface: the schema lives in one place, the .sql file, and everything downstream derives from it. Anywhere your project keeps two artifacts in sync by convention, ask which one should be the source.

Try today  VERDICT

If you write SQL from TypeScript, this is a same-day trial. It fails you if your SQL uses exotic dialect features a two-day-old parser has not met yet, and there is no track record to lean on, so pilot it on one service before rolling it across a codebase.

06  GitHub: 823 stars since Oct 11, MIT · arXiv 2610.14162 · image: HazyResearch

🧠 Next-Concept Modeling: draft the text, then sharpen it

Stanford’s HazyResearch generates text coarse-to-fine in parallel passes instead of one token at a time. Paper, code and weights, MIT, 823 stars.
SCENE

Every chatbot you use writes the way a typewriter does: one token, then the next, no going back. HazyResearch’s NCM starts from a rough sketch of the whole answer and refines it level by level, watching a sample sharpen from outline to full text in a few passes.

THE THING

The machinery is two-stage: a multiscale residual VQ tokenizer turns text into a hierarchy of discrete codes, coarse layers down to fine ones, and a transformer predicts that hierarchy top-down. Positions inside a level generate in parallel, so one forward evaluation buys a whole level. The repo landed October 11 with paper, code and a Hugging Face collection, and sat quietly until HN found it this week. Speed and quality claims are the authors’ own; the repo is research-grade, so expect paper-shaped code, not a serving stack.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the pattern, not the model: coarse-to-fine is how good drafters work, and it is a shape you can impose on any pipeline, including agent loops: sketch the plan cheaply, then spend tokens refining only where it matters.

Watch  VERDICT

Read the paper and the repo; pair it with today’s video below. It fails you if you need a drop-in endpoint today, and the fair comparison against strong autoregressive models is still the authors’ own benchmark tables.

07  GitHub: 583 stars, created Oct 22, license unclear · card: GitHub

💻 Snapintosh: macOS boots on a Snapdragon laptop

An EL2 hypervisor and toolchain running macOS on the ASUS A16, a Snapdragon X2 Elite Windows machine. 583 stars in its first day.
SCENE

Someone looked at a Snapdragon X2 Elite laptop, an ASUS A16 that ships with Windows, and decided the correct operating system for it was macOS. A day later the repo had 583 stars.

THE THING

Snapintosh is an EL2 hypervisor and toolchain that boots macOS on that hardware, Adreno GPU and all, the same class of Arm chip Apple’s own machines abandoned Intel for. The author is Acelogic, the repo appeared yesterday, and the license situation is unclear, which matters here. So does the legal one: macOS’s license agreement limits it to Apple-branded hardware, so this is a research artifact, not your next daily driver.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the audacity audit: the interesting question is never "can it boot" but "what had to be true about the hardware for this to take a day." Arm laptops are converging; the artificial walls between them are the story.

Watch  VERDICT

Star it and read the hypervisor code; do not plan a purchase around it. It fails you on driver completeness, on the license question, and on Apple’s terms, all three of which are outside the code’s control.
