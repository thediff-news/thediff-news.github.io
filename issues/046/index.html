01  NVIDIA Q3 FY27 results, Nov 17 · board chair Harvey C. Jones on the earnings call · SEC probe remains WSJ reporting

💰 Nvidia doubles revenue to $115.8 billion - and the board says Jensen Huang is not coming back.

Q3 FY27 revenue up 103% year over year, Vera Rubin NVL72 shipping at five-plus clouds, and a $130 billion Q4 outlook that assumes zero China data-center compute - plus a board statement that ends the comeback talk, on the record.
SCENE

First earnings since the resignation. Interim CEO Colette Kress ran the numbers; Harvey C. Jones, the company’s first board chair, opened with the statement everyone was waiting for. Ahead of the call, Huang had said he would be willing to return as CEO - per The Verge’s reporting. The board’s answer took one sentence.

THE THING

The verified facts, from the official release: revenue $115.8 billion, up 103% year over year and 20% quarter over quarter; Data Center $107.6 billion, up 110%; GAAP EPS $2.70. Vera Rubin NVL72 has shipped - “the fastest product ramp in the company’s history,” Nvidia says - with racks running at CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure and Nebius. The Q4 outlook: $130 billion, plus or minus 3% - and the release states Nvidia is not assuming any Data Center compute revenue from China in that outlook. Then the board statement, quoted in The Verge’s transcript: the board “unanimously accepted” Huang’s resignation and “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO”; Heidrick & Struggles is retained for the permanent CEO search, internal and external candidates; and the board has established a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee of independent directors, external advisors and legal counsel. The company declined to address or take questions on the circumstances of the resignation and the ongoing investigation. What remains reporting, not board-confirmed: the SEC probe (WSJ), and Huang’s December 1 testimony before lawmakers (The Verge).

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the guidance line. When the world’s largest chip supplier formally budgets zero data-center compute revenue from China, that is the export-control endgame priced in by the party with the most to lose - plan your capacity roadmap around restricted-market supply staying restricted, not blowing over. Second, Vera Rubin is now rentable: Google Cloud’s A5X instances are generally available and OCI has NVL72 systems and Vera CPUs in GA, per the release, so the next price-performance eval on your training and inference mix has new hardware in it. And one line worth noticing: Nvidia says it expanded Anthropic’s access to Grace Blackwell and Vera Rubin capacity through cloud providers - the IPO in story two is buying compute from story one.

Watch  VERDICT

Nothing to patch; a roadmap assumption to update. If you plan GPU capacity, the China-assumption line and the new oversight committee are the tells for how permanent the export regime is. This fails you if you read the board statement as closure - the SEC probe is still reporting, the internal investigation is still open, and the company declined all questions on both.

02  Anthropic roadshow announcement, Nov 10 · pricing expected Nov 18 US hours · above-range reports cite unnamed sources

🔔 Anthropic is expected to price its IPO today - reportedly at the top of its range, or above.

825,000,000 Class A shares at an expected $70 to $78, trading expected Thursday on Nasdaq under ANT. The above-range chatter is anonymous sourcing; the clock is not.
SCENE

Eight days ago Anthropic launched the roadshow; Reuters reported the book six times oversubscribed (No. 044). Sometime during US hours today, the price gets set. At our send time - Wednesday morning in Israel - it has not happened yet.

THE THING

The verified facts, from Anthropic’s own announcement: 825,000,000 shares of Class A common stock - 801,351,354 offered by Anthropic and 23,648,646 by existing stockholders, whose proceeds do not go to the company - plus a 30-day underwriters’ option on up to 123,750,000 more. Expected range: $70.00 to $78.00 per share. Listing applied for on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol ANT. Yesterday CNBC reported, citing unnamed sources, that Anthropic is likely to price above the range; QZ reported guidance to the top of the range or above, also unsourced by name. Treat those as directional chatter, not a set price. Trading is expected to begin Thursday.

✂️ STEAL IT

Steal the supply-chain reading. An IPO at this size is, functionally, a compute pre-purchase: the proceeds fund the training runs and capacity commitments that decide what Claude access looks like for the next few years - and Nvidia’s own release this week says Anthropic’s Grace Blackwell and Vera Rubin access through cloud providers was expanded. If Claude is load-bearing in your stack, Thursday’s debut tells you how public investors priced that plan; the pricing tonight tells you what the institutions actually paid.

Watch  VERDICT

Nothing to do before the number exists. Check the final price against the $70-78 range once Anthropic or the underwriters publish it - until then every figure above the range is anonymous sourcing. This fails you if you trade on the chatter, or if you mistake a strong debut for evidence about Claude’s product quality; it prices the compute plan, not the model.
